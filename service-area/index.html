What happens when you call from a town on this list

.

The dispatcher confirms the address, asks about the water source and the size of the wet area, and explains what the crew will do first. Distance is part of the conversation. A call from Wasco and a call from Lost Hills start the same way, but the drive is different, and you will hear an honest description of how the day looks.

Crews bring truck-mounted or portable extraction equipment, air movers, dehumidifiers and moisture meters to every job, so the work does not wait on a second trip for basic equipment.

Homes, farms and businesses

.

Calls from this area come from a mix of older single-story homes on slabs, newer subdivision houses, mobile and manufactured homes, packing sheds, shops and small commercial buildings. Each loses water differently. A slab home with a pipe leak under the floor is not the same job as a shop with a failed water heater or a farm outbuilding with a broken irrigation line.

When you call, say what kind of building it is. That helps the crew bring the right equipment and plan for the right materials.

What to know in Wasco and Kern County

.

Poso Creek runs near Wasco, and in March 2023 it prompted evacuations in the Pond area, a reminder that creek flooding is possible in this part of Kern County. FEMA flood maps and Kern County Public Works can tell you whether your address sits in a mapped flood zone. Standard homeowners policies generally do not cover flood, so check your own policy.
