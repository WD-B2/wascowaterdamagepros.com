Kitchen water damage: the cabinets take the hit

.

Kitchen water damage is moisture damage to cabinets, countertops, flooring and walls in the room where water lines, drains and appliances meet. Cabinet boxes made of particleboard swell and fall apart when wet, so a slow leak under a sink can wreck them before anyone sees water.

This is not always a middle-of-the-night call, but the 24/7 line is open if water is spreading across the floor.

Where kitchen leaks start

.

Most kitchen losses start under or behind something.

Sink supply and drain

Countertops usually survive. It is the cabinet base and the subfloor beneath that fail.

Floors in the kitchen

.

Tile and vinyl look fine when wet, and the plywood beneath can be soaked. A tile floor with a hollow sound or a loose plank is a sign of water below. See hardwood floor water damage repair for wood flooring behavior and structural drying for how readings are tracked.

Cabinets, countertops and the sink base

.

Water settles at the bottom of the cabinet, so the first parts to fail are the base, the toe-kick and the floor of the cabinet box. Doors and drawer fronts often survive. A countertop on a wet base can stay level for a while and then sag as the base weakens.

Crews remove the toe-kick and drill small holes to let air into the cabinet cavity, which avoids removing every cabinet. If the base is swollen, the affected cabinet is replaced instead of patched.

The dishwasher and refrigerator side of the kitchen

.

Dishwashers leak at the door seal, at the inlet valve and at the drain connection. Refrigerators leak at the ice maker line and the water filter housing. Both can run for days behind the appliance.

Pull the appliance only when the floor is dry enough to walk on, and look for a damp subfloor, a soft spot or a discolored baseboard. The appliance side of the problem is covered at appliance leak water damage.

What the S500 standard expects

.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry standard for water damage restoration. It expects the source to be identified, the water categorized, wet materials documented and drying verified with measurements. Those same records also support an insurance claim.

A note for Wasco kitchens

.

Homes near the original Deweyville townsite are likely to be older than those on the newer edges of town, and older kitchens often have galvanized or older copper supply lines. Ask the person who inspects your plumbing about the age and condition of the lines under the sink.
