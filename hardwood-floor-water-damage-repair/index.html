Hardwood floor water damage and the clock

.

Hardwood floor water damage happens when wood boards, engineered planks or laminate absorb water from a leak, a flood or high humidity. Wood is hygroscopic, which means it takes on moisture and swells, and the swelling pushes boards against each other and lifts the edges.

This is usually not a middle-of-the-night call, but the 24/7 line is open if water is on the floor right now. The sooner boards are dried, the better the chance they recover without replacement.

What wet wood floors look like

.

Different shapes mean different problems.

Cupping

.

Board edges rise higher than the centers. It usually comes from moisture below the boards.

Crowning

.

Centers rise above the edges, often after boards were sanded while still wet.

Buckling

.

Boards lift off the subfloor, a sign of heavy water.

Gaps and stains

.

Gaps open as boards dry and dark stains point to long exposure.

Scenario.

Hardwood damaged by a slow leak

.

A slow leak from a dishwasher or a water line under a floor wets boards from below. The first sign is often one board that cups or a dark ring that will not wipe away. By then the subfloor can be soaked.

A technician checks the boards, the subfloor and the neighboring rooms with a moisture meter. If the water is clean and the leak has stopped, drying systems often save more than homeowners expect. Refinishing versus replacing

.

Cupped boards that dry and flatten can often be sanded and refinished, which restores the surface. Boards that stay deformed, split or stained dark are replaced. Mixing new and old boards means stain matching, and it often makes sense to refinish the whole room for a uniform look.

Engineered wood has a thin top layer over plywood. It can be sanded only a few times, which affects the decision.

The standard behind the work

.

The reference standard for professional water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how water is categorized, how affected materials are judged and how drying is confirmed with readings. When you compare companies, ask whether the drying plan follows it and ask to see the readings.

Wood floors in valley homes

.

Wasco's dry summers mean wood floors are usually acclimated to low humidity. A sudden wet event stresses the boards more than a coastal home with steady humidity. NOAA normals show July and August with almost no rain, which makes indoor leaks the likelier cause of floor damage in summer.
