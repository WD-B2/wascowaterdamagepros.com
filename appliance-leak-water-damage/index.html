Appliance leak emergency: the water you did not see

.

Appliance leak water damage is damage from water that escapes a washing machine, dishwasher, refrigerator ice maker or similar appliance. It is an emergency when it is running, because the supply line keeps feeding it. The 24/7 line is open at night, on weekends and on holidays.

Close the shutoff valve behind or beside the appliance, or the main valve if you cannot reach it. Unplug the appliance only if the outlet is dry. Pull the machine out only if the floor is not slippery and you can do so safely.

Which appliance, which kind of water

.

The failure point changes the amount and the type of water.

Washing machine

.

Supply hoses and drain hoses can burst under pressure. A burst hose can run until someone turns it off.

Dishwasher

.

Door seals, supply lines and drain hoses leak under cabinets. Cabinet bases and the subfloor soak first.

Refrigerator with ice maker

.

A small copper or plastic line can leak behind the refrigerator for days.

Other units

.

Water softeners, humidifiers and in-wall units can leak into wall cavities.

Appliance leaking right now

.

Shut the supply valve and call the 24/7 line.

Slow leaks are the expensive ones

.

A split hose that bursts in front of you is dramatic. A slow drip behind a refrigerator is worse in some ways, because it saturates the subfloor for weeks before a soft spot or a smell gives it away.

If you notice a musty odor behind an appliance, a floor that flexes or a baseboard that has swollen, call before you pull the unit. A moisture meter and a thermal camera show how far the water traveled. See hardwood floor water damage repair when wood or laminate is involved.

Industry standard to ask about

.

Professional water damage work is guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which ties the plan to the category of water and the materials involved. A company that follows it can show you moisture readings from the first day and again when the structure is dry.
