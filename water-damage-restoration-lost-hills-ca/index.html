What goes wrong with water in a small desert-edge community

.

Lost Hills is a working community of homes, shops along the highway and businesses tied to agriculture and the oil field. Its water problems follow that mix.

Plumbing in the heat

.

Long, hot summers keep air conditioners and water heaters running hard. Supply lines, tank fittings and condensate drains fail under that load.

Shops and utility buildings

.

Commercial and agricultural buildings along the highway have roof drains and floor drains that can back up after winter rain.

Manufactured housing

.

If your home is a manufactured or mobile home, supply lines run under the floor and a leak can soak the underside before it shows inside.

Runoff

.

Highway and field runoff can carry water toward low spots after heavy storms, so check where your lot drains.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

.

These steps keep damage down while the crew makes the drive.

Stop the source

.

Shut the main water valve. If you cannot find it, close the valve at the appliance or fixture.

Cut power with care

.

Turn off breakers to wet rooms only if the panel is dry. If water is near the panel, call the power utility or 911 first.

Lift and move

.

Raise furniture legs, rugs and electronics out of the water and onto a dry surface.

Keep out of dirty water

.

Keep children and pets away from water that is dark or smells like sewage.

Call the line

.

Say which rooms are wet and when the water started. The more specific you are, the better the crew can plan.

Coverage, permits and who to ask

.

Lost Hills is not an incorporated city, so permit questions for larger repairs go to Kern County rather than to a city building department. Ask the county what applies to your parcel before a major rebuild.

Students in Lost Hills are served by the Wasco Union High School District, a small reminder that the community is tied to Wasco in more ways than the road. For the nearest larger town, see water damage restoration in Delano or McFarland.

The standard behind the work

.

The reference standard for professional water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how water is categorized, how affected materials are judged and how drying is confirmed with readings. When you compare companies, ask whether the drying plan follows it and ask to see the readings.
