Burst pipe emergency: do these in order

.

A burst pipe is a plumbing failure that puts pressurized water into a building, and it is an emergency in the plainest sense. The 24/7 line is open now, and a crew can be dispatched at night, on weekends and on holidays. Start with the water, not with the phone, if the shutoff is within reach.

Household supply pressure commonly sits between 40 and 80 psi. At only 5 gallons a minute, a broken line puts 300 gallons into a house in an hour. That is why the first act is closing the main valve, usually near the street side of the house or beside the water heater.

Safe first steps before the crew arrives

.

Do these only if you can reach them without standing in water near electricity.

  • Close the main water shutoff, then open a faucet at the lowest point to drain remaining pressure.
    • .
  • Turn off power to the wet area at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Otherwise call the power utility or 911 first.
    • .
  • Lift furniture legs, rugs and electronics out of the water and move small items to a dry room.
    • .
  • Take photos and short video of the break and the wet areas before anything is moved, for your insurance file.
    • .
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe where the pipe is, how long it ran and which rooms are wet.
    • .

Do not stand on wet floors near outlets, and do not try to repair the pipe yourself while the line is still live.

Where burst pipes tend to fail

.

The location of the break changes the cleanup plan.

Inside a wall

.

Water runs down the cavity and soaks insulation and the bottom of the drywall. Wall cavities often need flood cuts to dry.

Under a slab or floor

.

The leak may run for days before it shows. See slab leak water damage for that case.

In the attic

.

Water collects in insulation and then drops through the ceiling. See ceiling water damage repair for what follows.

At a fitting or valve

.

Often a smaller leak, but old compression fittings and corroded copper can fail suddenly.

How cleanup and drying work after a burst pipe

.

A burst line is clean water at first, which helps. The sequence is still the same as for other losses.

  2. Open what is wet

  3. Dry with equipment

  4. Plan the repair

Industry standard to ask about

.

Professional water damage work is guided by the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which ties the plan to the category of water and the materials involved. A company that follows it can show you moisture readings from the first day and again when the structure is dry.

Burst pipes in Wasco homes

.

Frost is not the usual cause here. NOAA climate normals show Wasco gets about 6.8 inches of rain a year, and summers are nearly dry, so most burst pipes are old connectors, corroded copper or high pressure that finally gives out. Homes near the original Deweyville townsite are likely to be older than the newer subdivisions, so ask whoever inspects your plumbing how old the lines are.

Ask your plumber about a pressure regulator and about replacing braided supply hoses that are many years old. Those two checks often cost far less than the cleanup they prevent.
