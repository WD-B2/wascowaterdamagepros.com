Flood damage cleanup starts with safety

.

Flood damage restoration is the cleanup of a building after outside water enters it, from rising creeks, overland runoff or storm drains that cannot keep up. In Wasco this is an emergency service and the 24/7 line takes calls at night and on weekends. The first step is not cleanup. It is getting people and pets out of moving or dark water.

If water is still entering, move to higher ground and wait for the all clear from local authorities. Do not turn on power in a flooded area, and call the power utility or 911 for downed lines or a gas smell. Once the house is safe to enter, call us, take photos and do not wade through standing water.

Why flood water is a contamination job

.

Outside water carries soil, fertilizer, farm runoff and sewage from overwhelmed systems. Industry practice under ANSI/IICRC S500 treats flood water as Category 2 or Category 3, which means porous materials such as carpet, pad and wet drywall are usually removed instead of saved.

That is the main difference from a plumbing leak. A clean burst pipe often lets materials be dried in place. Flood water usually means tear-out, sanitizing and drying the structure behind. See burst pipe water damage for that clean-water comparison.

What Wasco owners should check about flood risk

.

Poso Creek is the local flood story. In March 2023, Kern County Fire issued an evacuation order for the Pond area near Wasco during creek flooding, and debris at the State Route 43 bridge has been a known concern. That makes a flood in Wasco a real, local possibility and not only a coastal one.

For your own address, check the FEMA flood map, and ask Kern County Public Works, which publishes the flood insurance rate maps, or the City of Wasco building department about your lot. Do not assume a mapped zone does or does not apply until you have looked it up.

Silt, mud and what comes after the water

.

When creek water recedes it leaves a layer of fine silt on floors and in wall cavities. Silt dries to a crust and holds contaminants, and it is much harder to remove dry than wet. Removing it while it is damp, before drying equipment runs, saves time.

Appliances, HVAC ducts and electrical equipment that were under water need inspection before use. Ask a licensed electrician to check anything that was submerged.
