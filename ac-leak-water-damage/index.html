AC leak water damage in a hot-summer home

.

AC leak water damage is the wetting of building materials by condensate or ice melt from a cooling system. An air conditioner can pull gallons of water from humid air in a day, and that water is meant to leave through a drain line. When the line clogs, the water goes into the ceiling, a closet or the drywall instead.

Turn the AC off at the thermostat when you see a drip, because running it keeps making water. This is not usually a middle-of-the-night emergency, but the line is open 24/7 if water is spreading.

Where AC leaks start

.

The source tells you whether it is a quick fix or a hidden one.

Clogged condensate drain

.

Algae and debris block the line, and the pan overflows.

Rusted or cracked drain pan

.

Old pans in attics crack and leak with no warning.

Frozen evaporator coil

.

Low airflow or low refrigerant ices the coil, and melting ice floods the pan.

Disconnected or sagging line

.

A drain line that came loose can leak into the wall.

Attic and closet units hide the damage

.

Many homes in the valley have the air handler in the attic or a closet. A leak there shows up as a stain on the ceiling below or a soft spot on the wall beside it, often long after the first drips. The drywall can be wet and warm before the stain appears.

A moisture meter and thermal camera trace the wet path. If the ceiling is already sagging, treat it as a ceiling emergency. See ceiling water damage repair for the repair steps.

Why a frozen coil is a warning sign

.

When an evaporator coil freezes over, the unit is not moving enough air or has a refrigerant problem. As the ice melts, the drain pan overflows and the water goes into the building. If you find ice on the lines, turn the system off and let it thaw before calling an HVAC technician.

Running the unit to melt the ice does not fix the cause. Water damage follows when the thaw overwhelms the pan, so catching it before the thaw avoids the ceiling stain.

How the work is measured

.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the recognized reference for water damage restoration. Its core idea is simple: a structure is dry when readings say so, not when it feels dry. Ask for the drying log before you sign off on a job.

Why Wasco summers matter for AC leaks

.

NOAA normals for Wasco show July and August with almost no rain, so water appearing in summer comes from the house, not the sky. Air conditioners run long hours in the valley and condensate lines clog from the heavy use.

If a stain appears in a dry month, check the air handler before you call a roofer. A leak in the attic often turns out to be the drain line, not the roof.
