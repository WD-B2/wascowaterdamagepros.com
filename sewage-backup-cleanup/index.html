In plain words.

Water coming up out of a toilet or floor drain?

.

Water that rises from a toilet, shower or floor drain and smells foul is usually a sewage backup. It happens when a line clogs or a main overloads, and the contaminated water has nowhere to go but up. Searches for this often use symptom words like gurgling drains, toilet bubbling or water in the shower drain.

The trade calls it Category 3 water, and the cleanup is a contamination job before it is a drying job. The sections below explain what to do and what the crew does.

Sewage backup: what to do first

.

These steps keep people safe until the crew arrives.

  • Stop using sinks, toilets, showers and the washing machine. Every flush adds to the backup.
    • .
  • Keep children, pets and everyone else out of the water and away from wet areas.
    • .
  • If the water touches electrical outlets or a panel, stay out and call the power utility or 911 before calling us.
    • .
  • Do not try to clean it with a household wet-vac or mop and bucket, which can spread contamination.

  • Call the 24/7 line. Take photos from a safe distance before cleanup begins.

Why sewage backup is an emergency

.

Category 3 water carries bacteria and other contaminants. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard treats it as grossly contaminated and expects porous materials it touches, such as carpet, pad and drywall, to be removed. This is not a do-it-yourself cleanup.

The 24/7 line is open at night, on weekends and on holidays. Same-day response is the goal when crews are available, and there is no promised arrival time.

What you can and cannot save

.

Hard, nonporous items such as glazed tile, metal and sealed plastic can usually be cleaned and sanitized. Porous items that soaked up sewage water, including upholstered furniture, mattresses, books and stuffed toys, generally cannot be made safe again.

Photograph every item before it leaves the house and keep a list for your insurance adjuster. Crews bag and tag contaminated contents instead of carrying them through clean rooms.

Why the smell does not mean it is over

.

A sewage odor can linger in drywall, subfloors and wall cavities long after the visible water is gone. It is often the sign that porous material was missed, not that the cleanup is incomplete in spirit. Odor control is part of the job, and it follows removal and drying, not the other way around.

Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration help while work is underway. Once surfaces are clean and dry, the smell should be gone. If it returns, tell the crew and have the wall cavities rechecked.
