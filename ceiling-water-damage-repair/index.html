Ceiling water emergency: stop it, then trace it

.

Ceiling water damage is wet or stained drywall, plaster or texture caused by a leak above it. It is an emergency when the ceiling is dripping, bulging or sagging, because wet ceiling drywall can collapse under its own weight. The 24/7 line is open at night, on weekends and on holidays.

Move people, pets and furniture out from under a bulging ceiling. Put a bucket under any drip and, if a bulge is full of water, a small hole at its lowest point lets it drain into the bucket. Stay clear of light fixtures and wiring, and call the power utility or 911 first if water is near electrical fixtures.

Where ceiling water usually comes from

.

The stain is where the water showed up, which may be far from where it started.

The roof

.

Failed flashing, cracked shingles or a clogged valley let rain into the attic.

The attic

.

Condensation, an AC air handler or ducts can drip onto the ceiling. See AC leak water damage.

The room above

.

A tub, toilet or shower supply or drain leaking through the floor.

A supply line

.

Plumbing running through the ceiling cavity can fail.

Sagging ceiling, active drip

.

Move out from under it and call the 24/7 line.

Roof leaks.

Roof leak damage inside the house

.

Roof leaks have no page of their own on this site because the damage lands on the ceiling. Water that enters at the roof travels along rafters and insulation before it appears as a stain, often 5 to 10 feet from the actual hole. That is why a ceiling stain after a Wasco winter storm may not sit under the damaged shingle.

NOAA climate normals show Wasco gets roughly 5 of its 6.8 yearly inches of rain from December through March. Walk the attic with a flashlight after a storm and look for dark insulation, damp sheathing and daylight at penetrations. A roofer fixes the leak. Restoration dries the attic and the ceiling below so the leak does not turn into mold.

If the rain returns before the roof is repaired, ask a roofer about a temporary tarp. For the storm side of the problem, see storm water damage cleanup.

What the S500 standard expects

.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry standard for water damage restoration. It expects the source to be identified, the water categorized, wet materials documented and drying verified with measurements. Those same records also support an insurance claim.

What to do before help arrives

A wet ceiling can fail without warning.
