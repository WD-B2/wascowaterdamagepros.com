Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Wasco, CA

.

In plain words.

Wet walls and floors that never seem to dry on their own?

.

Searches for this usually sound like damp drywall after a leak, a floor that stays cold and wet, a musty smell that will not go away or a carpet that dried on top and stayed wet below. The surface feels dry, and the framing and subfloor are not.

The trade name for the fix is structural drying and dehumidification. It is the process of pulling moisture out of the building materials themselves, and it is what happens after the standing water is gone.

Water mitigation.

What water mitigation means

.

Water mitigation is the work done to stop water damage from getting worse. It includes stopping the source, extracting water, removing what cannot be saved and drying the structure. Structural drying is the longest part of it, and it is where the most mistakes happen when it is skipped or cut short.

Wasco has dry summers, and many people assume open windows and a fan will do the job. Dry outdoor air does not pull moisture out of a wall cavity or a subfloor fast enough, and trapped moisture is how mold gets a start. Professional drying uses controlled equipment and logged readings.

The equipment and what each piece does

.

A drying setup is sized to the loss, not stuffed with every machine.

LGR dehumidifiers

.

Low grain refrigerant units pull moisture from the air so wet materials keep releasing water.

Air movers

.

Axial and centrifugal units move air across wet surfaces and into wall cavities.

Moisture meters

.

Pin and pinless meters read how wet wood, drywall and concrete are.

Thermal imaging camera

.

Shows temperature differences that point to wet areas behind finishes.

Why we log readings

.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and it calls for monitoring drying so that equipment comes off when materials reach their dry standard. Ask any company whether its crews follow it and whether you will get a drying log. The log is the proof the structure is dry before walls are closed up or floors are laid.
