Older homes and newer homes lose water differently

.

Delano is an incorporated Kern County city of about 51,000 people, roughly 14 miles north-northeast of Wasco. Housing here ranges from older houses near the city's historic core to newer subdivisions on its edges. We cannot cite a primary source for the age mix, so ask your inspector about your own home.

In general, older houses carry older supply lines and drain pipes, and newer houses carry plastic fittings and appliance hoses that wear out on a different schedule. The first group tends to show leaks at old joints. The second tends to fail at flexible connectors, which is why a appliance leak or a failed hose is a common call.

Seasons: tule fog, winter rain and summer heat

.

Rain in the area is roughly 7.5 inches a year according to published climate summaries, so most calls come from inside the house.

Winter rain and fog

.

December through March brings the rain, and tule fog keeps the air damp. Roof leaks and wet walls dry slowly in that air.

Summer heat

.

Hot weather means long AC run times, and clogged drains and water heaters in garages are the likelier culprits.

Shoulder seasons

.

Spring and fall are good times to check gutters, flashing and water heater drain pans.

Anytime

.

Slow drying in damp fog

.

Tule fog keeps the winter air in the valley close to saturated for days. Wet drywall, carpet and framing release moisture slowly into air that is already humid, which is why a Delano winter job leans on dehumidifiers and not on open windows.

Crews log room humidity as well as material readings so the equipment is not pulled before the structure is dry.
